### 2.6 Synthesis

Regulation (EU) 2026/1738 counts its recovery targets in mass under Article 33. The same Regulation obliges
producers to supply information on the removal and replacement of parts and components under Article 11,
and to issue a Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport under Article 13. Alcoceba-Pascual et al. (2025) showed,
as section 2.2 sets out, that mass and thermodynamic value rank an electronic part very differently. The
target therefore gives a dismantler little reason to remove one, and the information duty is what could.
Adisorn et al. (2021) name the condition that decides whether it does. Whoever needs the information has to
have the equipment to reach it (p. 9), and has to reach it with the least possible effort (p. 10).

Where a passport has already met physical recovery work, the record has been read away from the object.
Plociennik et al. (2024) deliver it to a sensor-based sorting machine (p. 3), and Abdel-Aty et al. (2025)
to an operator at a computer beside the bench (p. 4). Section 2.5 shows the other half of the same problem.
Every augmented reality system reviewed there authors or senses its own instruction content. Mao et al.
(2025) come closest to closing the distance, fetching procedures from a structured knowledge base by
component reference into overlays anchored to the equipment (pp. 7, 8). That base is assembled inside one
operator's organisation, from its own maintenance history (p. 7). A recycler opening a unit for the first
time has neither.

Two questions stay open rather than resolved. Jansen et al. (2023) hold that the carrier these records are
built on is not proven suitable for arbitrary uses (p. 4), while Kühn et al. (2025) build a cross-industry
model on it and call the result a proof of concept awaiting experimental verification (p. 16). Against that,
Mao et al. (2025) report the only measured demand for the content that is missing, since 45 % of their
participants needed information about neighbouring subcomponents to finish a task (p. 13). What no study
has tested is a standardised record, issued with the product, read in the operator's field of view. Chapter
3 sets out how that record was structured, how the part was assessed, how the consequences of its recovery
routes were modelled, and how the join was tested.
