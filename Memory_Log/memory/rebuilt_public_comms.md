Created 2026-09-01 (Session 47), the first session after submission, when he asked for a LinkedIn
caption for the RBv2.1.1 demo video.

## The tagline, his own words

**"When an electronic device dies, ReBuilt is born."**

He wrote it and buried it at the bottom of his draft. It was moved to the first line, because on
LinkedIn only ~140 characters show on mobile before the "see more" cut, and that is the only line
most people read. Keep it as the hook in any future post.

Why: it is the strongest sentence he has written about the project, and it costs nothing to lead
with it.
How to apply: hook first, technical detail after the fold, the personal news last.

## The positioning sentence

ReBuilt is an Augmented Reality application that connects the Digital Product Passport to
end-of-life stakeholders, supports their dismantling tasks, and identifies components with high
environmental impact and high commercial value. **RBv2.1.1 is the minimum viable product** of that.

Reference product: Bosch Motorsport vehicle control unit, **MS 50.4** (space, matching the
datasheet and the thesis). The unit in the video is a **3D-printed teardown model, not the real
unit** and **never a replica** — the geometry deliberately does not match.

Next phase he states publicly: a true electronic unit, an AI agent layer, a robot arm assisting the
recycler, a human-machine framework.

## THE CLAIM TRAP, in public as in the thesis

His draft caption said the project lets us "face the challenge of increase the recycling rates of
critical raw materials". **That is the tenth catch of the "increase recycling rates" verb.**

Why: the thesis cannot support it. The gross environmental burden is identical across all four
modelled scenarios, and 8 of 9 participants said the material and recovery information did not help
the task. A public post is discoverable by an examiner before the defense.
How to apply: write the obstacle, never the gain. Approved public phrasing: *"That is where the
recovery of critical raw materials can start to move."* Aspiration, not result.

Also never claim in public: that dismantling decreases the unit's environmental impact; that the
tool increased recycling rates or measured engagement; that the headset was faster (it was slower);
per-part hazard highlighting (never implemented).

## His social-media assumption, corrected

He said "long text do not work in social media those days" and asked for something really short.

Why it matters: AuthoredUp's analysis of 372,126 LinkedIn posts (Sept 2025 to Feb 2026) puts the
engagement peak at **1,301 to 2,500 characters**, about **27 percent above posts under 400
characters**. His own draft was **979 characters** — already on the short side for the platform.
How to apply: the fix for a weak post is the hook and the line breaks, not the word count. Do not
cut his technical detail to chase brevity unless he insists after seeing the data.

## The two captions delivered

- **Option A, 948 characters** — his content restructured, tagline as hook, "Thesis submitted.
  Defense next." as the closing line. This was the recommendation.
- **Option B, 597 characters** — the genuinely short version he asked for, same hook, technical
  detail compressed to two lines.

Both are in the session transcript. Neither was confirmed as posted.

## Mechanics worth repeating for any video post

- Burn or upload subtitles: LinkedIn video is watched muted by default.
- Hook must land inside 140 characters (mobile cut) and 210 (desktop).
- One idea per paragraph with blank lines between; a wall of text loses the "see more" click.
- Put deep technical detail and any link in the first comment, not the caption body.

# FINAL CAPTION HE CHOSE, 2026-09-01 (1,103 characters)

When an electronic device dies, ReBuilt is born!

RBv2.1.1 is the prototype from my master's thesis, and the minimum viable version of what I want ReBuilt to become: an Augmented Reality application to connect the Digital Product Passport with end-of-life stakeholders, help them in their dismantling tasks, and identify the components worth recovering, the ones with high environmental impact and high commercial value.

The reference product is a Bosch Motorsport vehicle control unit, the MS 50.4. It is what structures the passport and the life cycle assessment behind it. The unit handled in the video is a 3D-printed teardown model, used to show how the application would work on a real unit.

The next stage of the prototype is to implement it on a true electronic unit, add an AI agent layer and a robot arm to assist the recycler, creating a human-machine framework.

That is where the recovery of critical raw materials can start to move.

Special thanks to my supervisors, Prof. Elle Langer and Prof. Dr. Saman Ghobadian, for the guidance during this period.

Thesis submitted. Defense next!
