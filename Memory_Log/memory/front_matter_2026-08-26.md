### Three substantive flags on paragraph 3

1. **"primary exploration" is a false friend.** In English, exploration means searching for a

   swap above. Keep "manifesto" if you want it; just know what it costs.
3. **"minimum viable product" is a business term.** It is fine in a foreword. It does not belong in
   any other chapter, where the prototype is RBv2.1.1.

Nothing in them says why an engineer chose this and not something else, and the Foreword is the only
page in the thesis where you are allowed to say it. Two or three sentences would close that gap. Your
call, and the section is not mandatory in the first place.

## The logical chain, one line per move

**1. Problem.** End-of-life electronics carry critical raw materials · the operator at the bench does
not know what is inside the housing or where it sits · that missing knowledge is the obstacle.
· Rests on: Restrepo et al. (2019, p. 2), the same source and page already used in Chapter 1 and 6.1.
· Do NOT write the remedy here. The literature does not endorse manual disassembly as the fix.

**2. Objective.** State the main question as a purpose, not as a question. It has two halves, both of
which must survive the compression: assist a recycler in disassembling a Vehicle Control Unit, and
assist in assessing the environmental consequences of its end-of-life routes.
· Trap: the main question is the ONE place "routes" is deliberately kept. Do not sweep it to
"scenarios" here.

**3. Scope and content.** A Digital Product Passport delivered through Augmented Reality on a
head-mounted display · a Vehicle Control Unit as the object · one prototype, ReBuilt · a printed
teardown model, which is NOT a replica of the reference unit.
· Trap: never let "replica" or "reproduces the geometry" into this page. Section 1.3 still says it and
that is a live defect, not a precedent.

**4. Methodology.** Two strands, and the abstract has to make them one project, not two.
· Strand A: cradle-to-grave life cycle assessment in openLCA on ecoinvent, four end-of-life scenarios,
EF 3.1 characterization, Monte Carlo uncertainty, cross-checked against ReCiPe 2016 midpoint.
· Strand B: within-subject test, nine volunteers, the Augmented Reality application against a
two-dimensional manual, measuring completion time, errors and perceived usability.
· The join that makes them one project, and the sentence most likely to be missing from your first
draft: the life cycle results are the passport's payload. The application displays them. Say this or
the abstract reads as two theses stapled together.

**5. Findings.** Four results, in this order. The order matters: the honest one goes first so the
positive one is not read as the headline.
· Time: no advantage. Five of the eight participants who ran both conditions were slower with the
headset.
· Errors: nothing to compare. No error occurred in either condition.
· Usability: the application is ahead. Higher for seven of the nine participants.
· Environment: guided dismantling (Sc3) avoids 3.22 times as much resource use, minerals and metals as
bulk shredding (Sc2), and 1.85 times as much climate impact. Adding component reuse (Sc4) raises those
to 4.74 and 3.49.
· 🔴 Every one of those four multiples needs "under EF 3.1 characterization" attached, in the same
sentence, once, covering all four.
· 🔴 The gross burdens of the four scenarios are identical. What changes is the avoided primary
production. If the abstract implies dismantling lowers the unit's impact, it contradicts your own
Table 8.
· If space runs out, the single sentence that must survive is that the three measures disagree, and
that the disagreement is the finding.

**6. Discussion and limits.** Pick ONE limitation. The strongest candidate, and the one Chapter 5
already carries: the teardown model was designed to come apart, so it removed the physical resistance
that decides whether reuse is possible, and that same property explains the absence of errors.
· Second candidate if you prefer it: the scenario ordering is robust while the absolute values are
not.
· Do not list both. There is no room.

**7. Conclusion.** What the work shows: the record structure is satisfiable and the delivery channel
works. What it does not show: that reading the record in the field of view makes the work faster or
recovers more material. Say both. The second half is what makes the first half credible.

**8. Keywords.** Not counted in the 300. Candidates, pick five or six:
Digital Product Passport · Augmented Reality · life cycle assessment · selective disassembly ·
critical raw materials · end-of-life electronics · Vehicle Control Unit.

impact". Each has been caught before, the last one eight times. Write the obstacle, never the gain.
