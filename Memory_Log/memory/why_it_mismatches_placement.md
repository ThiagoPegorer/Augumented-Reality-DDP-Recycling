# Where the "why" goes

Your question offers two homes. There are three, and the third is the one that solves it.

---

## The split

| what | where | why |
|---|---|---|
| What kg Sb eq and kg Cu eq each measure | **Methodology**, the impact assessment subsection | Description of a method. Methodology owns the methods, and it already describes both |
| The normalisation references and the characterised values | **Findings**, after the ranking | Numbers read off the model. Already drafted |
| Why the two disagree, and what that costs the result | **Discussion** | Interpretation. The Findings contract forbids it |

The Findings contract is explicit: no comments or interpretation of any kind. The boundary test is
mechanical. A sentence carrying "because", "which suggests", "this shows that" or "better than" has
crossed into Discussion.

---

## The test applied to real candidate sentences

Legal in Findings:

> ReCiPe normalizes mineral resource scarcity against 1.201 x 10^5 kg Cu eq. It normalizes
> freshwater ecotoxicity against 25.17 kg 1,4-DCB. The characterized Sc1 result for mineral resource
> scarcity is 2.203 kg Cu eq.

Not legal in Findings:

> Mineral resource scarcity ranks last **because** its normalisation reference is four orders of
> magnitude larger than the ecotoxicity references.

Same content. The first states three measurements and lets the reader join them. The second joins
them for the reader, and the join is the interpretation.

**You already have the explanation in Findings.** The three-number paragraph is the mechanism. It
does not announce itself as one, which is why it feels like something is missing.

## Discussion scaffold, the "why" block

Not prose. Notes and the logical chain, so the sentences are yours.

**Inherits from Findings:** the ranking, the three normalisation references, the ratio table, the
two divergences (minerals 1.245, human toxicity non-cancer 1.554).

**Hands to:** the limitations passage, and then to the design recommendation about what a passport
should display.

**Chain, in order:**

1. **Open on the disagreement as a finding about methods, not about the device.** Both methods rank
   the four scenarios identically. They disagree on magnitude in two of fifteen pairs.
2. **Name what agreement means here.** Ordering survived a change of characterisation factors.
   Magnitude did not, in the minerals pair. Those are two different robustness claims and the thesis
   can only make the first one strongly.
3. **The normalisation ranking is a statement about references, not about burdens.** The device's
   characterised mineral result is not small. The reference it is divided by is large. State that
   this is why ReCiPe normalisation was rejected as a selection basis, and that your own audit
   recorded the reason at the time rather than after the result was known.
   `[This is a genuine strength. Say it plainly, once.]`
4. **The two indicators are not measuring the same thing.** Antimony equivalents against copper
   equivalents. `[CITATION NEEDED, as above.]`
5. **Name the larger divergence you did not report.** Human toxicity non-cancer differs by 1.554 at
   Sc4, wider than minerals at 1.245. It falls outside the three reported categories, so it is not
   in Findings. Saying so here costs one sentence and closes the gap a reader would otherwise find.
6. **Land on the consequence for the passport.** The prototype displays single characterised values.
   Two published methods disagree by a quarter on one of the three headline categories. That is a
   design problem for what a passport shows a non-specialist, and it feeds the fifth objective.

**Length target:** about 200 words. Two paragraphs. Point 6 is the one that earns the block its
place in a thesis about a passport rather than in an LCA report.
