# 5.1 LCA model discussion, draft v3 (Session 43, 2026-08-26)

Built on Thiago's spine: selectivity creates the value, information is the precondition, the passport
carries the information. Eight paragraphs, each opening on an explicit interpretive claim.

PROSE. At risk for affidavit denoting.

---

The environmental value of an end-of-life scenario is created by selectivity, and selectivity depends
on information. The life cycle results establish the first half of that. Under EF 3.1
characterization, the scenarios that remove identified components before shredding avoid
substantially more primary production than bulk practice, and the avoided impact rises with every
step toward a more selective method. The model does not say how a recycler achieves that selectivity.
It says what achieving it is worth. Knowing which components carry the burden, what they are made of,
and how to remove them is the precondition, and that is the information a Digital Product Passport is
built to carry.

The passport cannot make the device cleaner. Under EF 3.1 characterization the four scenarios are
identical until the final stage, and that stage holds less than half of one percent of any result.
Almost the whole mineral burden is fixed when the device is manufactured, and nothing done at end of
life reaches it. What guidance decides is how much of that burden is recovered rather than destroyed.
A small share of the footprint is therefore the whole of the recycler's influence, and it is won or
lost in preprocessing, since a substance sent to the wrong output stream there is lost at the final
recovery step (Chancerel et al., 2009).

The value comes from selectivity and stops there. Removing identified components before shredding
produces the entire gain. The most selective scenario modeled here goes further and adds component
reuse, and it carries the widest uncertainty band in every reported category, because the rate at
which removed components pass testing for reuse is quantified nowhere in the literature.
Alcoceba-Pascual et al. (2025) met the same boundary from the other direction, since removing two
parts from a vehicle lifted recovery sharply while dismantling those parts further gained two
percentage points on one and slightly worsened the other. Reuse is a promise. Guided dismantling is a
result.

Transparency only helps if it names the right components. Resource use, minerals and metals holds
close to three quarters of this unit's weighted footprint under EF 3.1, and a very small mass of
material creates it. Regulation (EU) 2026/1738 counts its recovery targets in mass under Article 33,
so a dismantler meeting that target has no reason to remove a component this small. Compliance and
environmental benefit point in opposite directions for this device. The information duty under
Article 11 and the passport required under Article 13 are what could align them, and they can only do
so if the record carries impact at component level rather than mass alone.

This study ranks the scenarios. It does not price them. The four gross distributions overlap one
another completely, so the scenarios cannot be told apart on the burden they create, and the reported
gross climate result sits almost a fifth below the median of its own distribution. The credits behave
better and separate from one another, with one exception, since the guided dismantling and reuse
intervals meet in freshwater eutrophication. A passport can therefore tell a recycler which scenario
is better. It cannot say how much better, and nothing in this thesis rests on a claim that it can.

A passport that displays one method's ranking makes a policy choice on the reader's behalf. Expressed
as multiples of each method's own bulk-shredding credit, the two characterization methods agree in
climate change and in freshwater eutrophication, so the ordering of the scenarios is a property of
the model. The priority among categories is not. Minerals ranks first of sixteen under the
Environmental Footprint method and last of eighteen under the cross-check method, because the
reference values the two normalize against differ by four orders of magnitude. Whichever method a
record carries decides which category the person reading it is told to care about, and the record has
to say which one it used.

Every limit that mattered was a limit of data, not of method. A difference smaller than the declared
input ranges is not a result, and the gross comparison between scenarios is exactly such a
difference. The reuse rate is an assumption, and it produced the widest band in the study. The single
system model used across the background database already credits part of the benefit of recycling, so
the reported credits are an upper bound rather than an exact value, and burdens and credits are never
combined into one number, so that each can be weighed on its own.

A model that rates a scenario highly is not saying a person should perform it. This model charges
dismantling electricity and a share of a manual treatment facility, and nothing else that manual work
costs. Oke and Potgieter (2024, p. 6) report low disassembly efficiency and operator exposure to
capacitor explosions and poisonous gases, concluding that manual dismantling "fails to meet the
prerequisites of safe production". Guided dismantling is the scenario this model prefers and the one
a person is least equipped to perform unaided. What closes that distance is not a better model. It is
what the operator is shown while doing the work.
