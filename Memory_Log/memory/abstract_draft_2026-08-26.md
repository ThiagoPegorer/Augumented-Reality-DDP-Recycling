## ABSTRACT

A recycler opening an end-of-life electronic device rarely knows what its components are made of or
where they sit inside the housing, and that gap obstructs recovery of critical raw materials. This
thesis examines how a Digital Product Passport delivered through Augmented Reality can assist a
recycler in disassembling a Vehicle Control Unit and in assessing the environmental consequences of
its end-of-life routes.

A prototype, ReBuilt, was built for a head-mounted display. It anchors the passport beside the
physical unit, shows component-level material and impact data, and guides a five-step disassembly on
a printed teardown model. The environmental data it displays comes from a cradle-to-grave life cycle
assessment of the reference unit, built in openLCA on ecoinvent, covering four end-of-life scenarios
under EF 3.1 characterization, with a Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis and a cross-check against
ReCiPe 2016 midpoint. It was tested against a conventional two-dimensional manual with nine
volunteers, measuring task completion time, errors and perceived usability.

The three measures disagree, and that disagreement is the main result. Five of the eight participants
who performed both conditions were slower with the headset. No error occurred in either condition.
Seven of the nine rated the application higher, and every comparative item favored it. Under EF 3.1
characterization, guided dismantling avoids 3.22 times as much resource use, minerals and metals as
bulk shredding does, and 1.85 times as much climate impact, rising to 4.74 and 3.49 when component
reuse is added. The gross burden is identical across all four scenarios; only the avoided primary
production changes.

The teardown model was designed to come apart, which also explains the absence of errors. The
prototype shows that such a record can be structured and read on the object itself. It does not show
that reading it there makes disassembly faster or recovers more material.

**Keywords:** Digital Product Passport, Augmented Reality, life cycle assessment, selective
disassembly, critical raw materials, Vehicle Control Unit
