# 🔴 THE CITATION RULING FOR THE CONCLUSION, settled from the template 2026-08-26

He asked whether the Conclusion may carry citations. **It must.** The SRH template's own quotation
guidelines, verbatim:

> *"Throughout your thesis, it is outmost imperative that you cite every sentence which you didn't
> author!"* and *"Throughout your thesis, every text element that did not fully originate from your
> mind must be verifiable for the reader through your referencing."*

The list of what must be cited includes **"Legal statutes, regulations, (corporate) guidelines"**. The
plagiarism advisory on the following page says incorrect citation can lead to exmatriculation. The
only exemption named is common knowledge and general definitions in the primary subject area.

**Consequence:** his own conclusions carry no citation; the regulation dates, Restrepo, Oke and
Potgieter, Maurice and Chen all do. The artificial intelligence paragraph is safe unsourced **only
because it states outright that it is the author's expectation and not a finding.**

# 🔴 VOICE RULE 14, new 2026-08-26

**In the Conclusion, short clipped sentences read as disconnected.** His words: *"in a conclusion,
small sentences looks like sentences not connected... use more simple words and sentences more
clear."* The short-sentence discipline of rule 5 belongs to the argument chapters. The Conclusion
takes plainer vocabulary and longer joins, so each sentence carries the next. This does not license
stacked clauses; it licenses connectives.

# 🔴 THREE CLAIMS CORRECTED ON SUBSTANCE, not style

1. **"you can decrease significantly the environmental impact" by dismantling.** FALSE against his own
   Table 8: the gross burden is identical across all four scenarios to the fourth decimal place. What
   rises is the avoided primary production. Replaced.
2. **"increase recycling rates" / "the key to increase the material recovery."** The verb pattern,
   now at **eight catches**. He narrowed his own defence usefully: his claim is about *having product
   information*, not about manual disassembly. That version IS supportable, but only as an obstacle
   claim: Restrepo et al. (2019, p. 2) report the composition of vehicle electronics is poorly
   understood and that this hampers recycling strategies for critical raw materials in cars. **Nobody
   measured that supplying information raises a recovery rate.** Write the obstacle, never the gain.
3. **"by 2027 it will affect all the sectors."** No source anywhere on disk, and it contradicts his
   own Chapter 2 for the automotive sector. Replaced with the verified dates: Article 11 from
   September 2029, Article 33 from January 2030, Article 13 from September 2032.
