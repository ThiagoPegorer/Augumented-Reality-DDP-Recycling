# THE STORY SPINE, v2, HIS ORDER WITH THE ASSESSMENT BEFORE THE PROTOTYPE

Revised 2026-09-16 on his ruling that the openLCA methodology, results and discussion come before the
Augmented Reality prototype. 18 slides, **exactly 20:00**. This replaces both earlier spines. The
argument, the five findings to front-run and the demo protocol in [[defense-storyline]] still hold.

**Why this order is right, and it is not only narrative preference.** The thesis's own logical work
order is SQ1, SQ4, SQ2, SQ3: what information is relevant, what the environmental difference is, how
it is delivered through the interface, how it compares against a manual. The presentation order in
the paper is 1, 2, 3, 4 because a document has to be read in question order. **A talk does not.**
Telling it in work order means nothing on screen depends on something the audience has not seen yet.

**The one thing that order costs, and the fix.** Four minutes of system boundaries and functional
units before the audience has seen the artifact leaves them with nothing to hang it on. So the
artifact is revealed early and briefly, and the demo stays late. Slide 7 is one look at the thing,
forty seconds, no demo. The audience then knows what the numbers are for. The demo returns after the
results as the payoff: the value they just spent four minutes on, standing on the component in front
of a recycler.

# 🔴 WHAT MOVING THE DEMO LATE COSTS

The demo now lands around minute eleven instead of minute seven. If it fails there, he is mid-talk
with nine minutes left and an audience that was promised a demo on slide 7.

**So the abort discipline stops being optional.** Headset on, running and casting before slide 1.
One scripted path. A phone timer in his eyeline. **If it is not working by 40 seconds, he says the
rehearsed sentence and plays the fallback video**, which is loaded on the next slide and ready.
Recording that video is a Thursday task, not a Friday one.
