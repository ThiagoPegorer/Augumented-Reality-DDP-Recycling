## Three deliberate omissions, each with a reason

**The word for the midpoint is not used.** You confirmed 1 and 5. Nothing records what 3 was labelled
on the form, so the responses are reported in three bands and the section never says a 3 means
"neither". Counting is safe; naming is not.

**The instrument is still not named.** The paragraph reports a ten-item questionnaire, a five-point
scale and a 0 to 100 score, and makes no benchmark comparison. That costs one sentence and no
citation. Naming it obliges you to cite the source and then a reader expects the published benchmark
band, which is a second citation and a comparison you have not made.

**No chart for the comparative items.** With 78 of 90 cells at the same value, a bar chart draws a
distribution that is not in the data. The table carries it and takes less space.

---

## 🔴 One thing you should decide before this goes in

**Five of nine participants answered 5 on all ten comparative items, and 86.7 % of all responses are
5.** Reported as above it is honest, and the numbers are in the text where a reader can see them.

But a reader who sees ten items returning the same median from the same five participants will ask
what the ten items separated. [Likely] the answer is that they separated P02 and nobody else. That is
a limitation of the instrument on this sample, and it belongs in the Discussion beside the absent
errors — the same shape of finding, where the task was not demanding enough for the measure to move.

The alternative is to report the ten items as one block and stop giving them ten rows of table. I do
not recommend it: the per-item table is the evidence that the agreement is real rather than asserted,
and hiding the uniformity to make the result look better-differentiated is the wrong trade.
