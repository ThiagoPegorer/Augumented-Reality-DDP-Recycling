# [P] Verified 2026-08-17 against the publisher PDF, all 14 pages read

Jensen, S. F., Kristensen, J. H., Adamsen, S., Christensen, A., & Waehrens, B. V. (2023). Digital product
passports for a circular economy: Data needs for product life cycle decision-making. Sustainable
Production and Consumption, 37, 242-255. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spc.2023.02.021
Open access, CC BY 4.0. Online 4 Mar 2023. Aalborg University plus Grundfos A/S. Funded by MADE,

# THE FINDING THAT CONTRADICTS THE ReBuilt DESIGN

Section 4.5 (pp. 250-252) and Fig. 6 (p. 251): third-party recycling companies need FOUR data points.

| data point | Jensen's category |
|---|---|
| Material composition (MC) | priority |
| List of hazardous substances (LH) | priority |
| Guidelines for non-destructive disassembly (GD) | priority |
| Waste sorting guidelines (WG) | focused development (low availability, high importance) |

Verbatim, p. 251: "As shown in Fig. 6, data about material composition, a list of hazardous substances
and guidelines for non-destructive disassembly are considered priority data, while waste sorting
guidelines require additional work and are thus categorised as focused development data."

ENVIRONMENTAL FOOTPRINT IS ABSENT. Not in Fig. 6, and the recycler column of Table 5 (p. 247) is blank
for it. It appears only under Customer, Manufacturer and Supplier.
The ReBuilt passport shows an environmental impact tab to a recycler. The best multi-actor empirical
study of recycler data needs found recyclers did not ask for it.

Their explanation, p. 252, verbatim: "third-party recycling companies experience less uncertainty as the
trajectory of materials is pre-determined, particularly in the case of mechanical recycling, as material
fractions are shredded and thoroughly sorted afterwards. Consequently, only a few types of data would
support recycling companies in conducting high-quality recycling."

# RULING, taken 2026-08-17: where the departure is owned

Three places, each doing what that chapter is for. DO NOT COLLAPSE THEM.
1. Section 3.1 names in ONE sentence that a mismatch exists. No detail, no defence. (DONE)
2. Section 3.2.2 shows exactly which fields match Jensen and which do not. Descriptive.
3. The Discussion chapter defends the addition against the thesis's own results. A defence written
   before results exist is an assertion, which is why it does not live in Methodology.

The defence's strongest material is Jensen's own stated limitation, p. 253: the data needs "support
circularity at the actor level; they do not monitor or improve sustainability at a higher level."
Jensen's frame is actor-level decision support. The thesis adds the environmental consequence of the
route chosen. They did not test the second because they did not ask about it.

# WHY THE OTHER THREE POINTS HELP

Verbatim, p. 251: "recycling companies often end up spending a significant amount of time disassembling
products and sorting materials themselves before they are shredded. Being able to access disassembly
guidelines and material composition in a digital product passport would enable recycling companies to
generate even cleaner material fractions."
Jensen's recycler, at the disassembly step, IS the thesis's operator. Three of their four priority items
are exactly what ReBuilt delivers.
Also p. 251: "The lack of data about material composition is a prevalent challenge for recycling
companies, particularly in the case of plastics", and increased transparency enables "the swift
identification of hazardous substances (e.g., from batteries)".

# METHOD, usable as a precedent and against the replication standard

- Design: multiple-case study following Yin (2009), theory-building per Eisenhardt and Graebner (2007).
  Three circular supply chains, each with supplier, manufacturer, service provider, customer and
  third-party recycler. All Danish, all large producers of MECHATRONIC products (p. 245).
- Case selection criteria (p. 245): (1) actively engaged in circular transition and exploring end-of-life
  value retention; (2) products for which a DPP is expected to become legally mandated; (3) same industry.
- Phase 1 (p. 246): 28 semi-structured interviews, online, recorded and transcribed, companies
  anonymised. Total 14 h 20 m. Respondents and durations in Table 3, p. 245. Conventional content
  analysis per Hsieh and Shannon (2005), coded against the seven clusters from the literature (Table 2).
- Phase 2 (p. 246): survey to the interviewees. N = 22, 71 % response. Each data point rated on
  five-point Likert scales on three parameters: importance, availability, sensitivity.
- Sensitivity bands, Fig. 1 p. 246: Low < 2.5, Medium 2.5-3.5, High > 3.5.
- Sampling: convenience and snowball, per Taherdoost (2016). They call the output "indicative results
  from a mechatronic industry", p. 246.

# THE FRAMEWORK, reusable in the thesis

28 data points in seven clusters (p. 244, restated p. 253): (1) usage and maintenance, (2) product
identification, (3) products and materials, (4) guidelines and manuals, (5) supply chain and reverse
logistics, (6) environmental data, (7) compliance.

Four groups from importance x availability (p. 246): OUT OF SCOPE = low availability, low importance.
SUPPORTIVE = high availability, low importance. FOCUSED DEVELOPMENT = low availability, high importance.
CRITICAL / PRIORITY = high availability, high importance.

Their research question, p. 243: "What are the critical decision points and data needs for actors in a
circular supply chain to support product life cycle decision-making?"

Table 6, p. 252 compares their 28 points against Adisorn et al. (2021), Berger et al. (2022), European
Commission (2022), Goetz et al. (2022) and the Danish Business Authority (2021).

# THEIR OWN LIMITATIONS, p. 253, quote these when using the source

- Situated in the mechatronics industry; "data needs are likely to differ across industries, albeit with
  a share of similarities, which brings their generalisability into question."
- "authorities were not included despite their important role in conducting market surveillance."
- The data needs "support circularity at the actor level; they do not monitor or improve sustainability
  at a higher level."

# VERIFICATION NOTES

- The paper is a PRIMARY empirical study, not a review, so verification rule C does not bite.
- Multi-measure caution (rule B): every data point carries THREE ratings. Never report importance alone.
  Fig. 6's recycler points sit at roughly importance 3.5-4 and availability around 3, but those
  coordinates are read off a scatter plot, not printed as numbers. A coordinate quoted from a figure is a
  DERIVED value under rule A. Use the categories, not the numbers.
- Mechatronic products (power conversion, cooling and heating, water pumps) are a reasonable analogue for
  a Vehicle Control Unit, but write that as a judgement, not as something the paper states.
